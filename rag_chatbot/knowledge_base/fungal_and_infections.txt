CLINICAL GUIDELINE: CUTANEOUS FUNGAL, BACTERIAL & VIRAL INFECTIONS

1. DERMATOPHYTE INFECTIONS (TINEA)
Infections caused by dermatophyte fungi (Trichophyton, Microsporum, Epidermophyton) digesting stratum corneum keratin.
- Tinea Corporis (Ringworm): Annular (ring-shaped) erythematous plaque with active elevated, scaly advancing border and central clearing.
- Tinea Cruris ("Jock Itch"): Pruritic erythematous plaques in the groin and upper thighs, sparing the scrotum (crucial distinction from cutaneous candidiasis which involves the scrotum and has satellite pustules).
- Tinea Pedis (Athlete's Foot): Interdigital maceration, fissuring, scaling on soles ("moccasin distribution").
- Tinea Capitis: Scaling, patchy alopecia with broken "black dot" hairs on scalp. Requires systemic oral griseofulvin or terbinafine (topical therapy is ineffective for follicular hair shaft infection).
- Diagnostic Confirmation: KOH wet mount microscopy demonstrating branching hyphae.

2. PITYRIASIS VERSICOLOR (TINEA VERSICOLOR)
Superficial infection by Malassezia globosa/furfur.
- Presentation: Hypopigmented or hyperpigmented, finely scaling macules coalescing on chest, back, and shoulders. More visible after sun exposure.
- Management: Topical selenium sulfide shampoo, ketoconazole 2% cream, or oral fluconazole.

3. BACTERIAL INFECTIONS
- Impetigo: Highly contagious superficial Staphylococcus aureus / Streptococcus pyogenes infection. Honey-colored crusts around mouth and nose in children. Treatment: Topical mupirocin 2% or oral cephalexin.
- Folliculitis: Erythematous follicular pustules centered on hair shafts.
- Cellulitis: Spreading erythema, warmth, edema, and tenderness of deep dermis and subcutaneous tissue. Systemic fever often present; requires systemic antibiotics.

4. VIRAL DERMATOSES
- Herpes Simplex (HSV-1 / HSV-2): Grouped painful vesicles on an erythematous base ("dewdrops on a rose petal"), transitioning to crusts. Oral valacyclovir or acyclovir.
- Herpes Zoster (Shingles): Reactivation of Varicella Zoster Virus along a single unilateral dermatome. Severe prodromal neuropathic pain preceding vesicular eruption. Early antiviral initiation within 72 hours reduces post-herpetic neuralgia risk.
- Verruca Vulgaris (Warts): Human Papillomavirus (HPV) induced hyperkeratotic, exophytic papules with thrombosed capillary black dots. Cryotherapy or salicylic acid.
